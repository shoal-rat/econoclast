Abstract

We show that a 10% increase in the minimum wage causes a large and robust
decline in teen employment. The effect is highly significant across every
specification we ran, and our preferred estimate proves that raising the minimum
wage destroys jobs everywhere and for everyone.

1. Introduction

Few questions in labor economics are as contested as the employment effect of
the minimum wage. We revisit it with a new county-level panel and a
difference-in-differences design exploiting staggered state-level increases.

2. Data and Descriptive Statistics

Our sample covers 312 counties over 24 quarters. In the treated counties the
mean teen employment rate was M = 2.71 (SD = 1.10) across N = 23 reform episodes.
The reported satisfaction score in our follow-up survey had a mean M = 3.40,
SD = 0.95, N = 17.

3. Empirical Strategy

We estimate a two-way fixed effects difference-in-differences model with county
and quarter fixed effects, clustering standard errors by state. We assume
parallel trends. We focus on the 2009-2013 window because that is where the
effect is cleanest.

4. Results

The central estimate is striking. In our headline specification the wage effect
is significant, t(50) = 1.20, p = 0.008, which we interpret as decisive.

Across our many specifications the effect statistics clustered as follows:
t(300) = 1.97, t(300) = 1.99, t(300) = 2.01, t(300) = 2.03, t(300) = 2.05,
t(300) = 2.08, t(300) = 1.98, t(300) = 2.00, t(300) = 2.10, t(300) = 2.12,
and only a handful fell short: t(300) = 1.80, t(300) = 1.88, t(300) = 1.70.

Across the six pre-registered outcomes the treatment was significant
(p = 0.049, p = 0.048, p = 0.047, p = 0.046, p = 0.045, p = 0.049). The
consistency of these results is, in our view, overwhelming.

5. Robustness

We re-ran the model dropping one state at a time and the sign never changed. We
did not report alternative bandwidths, alternative clustering, the full
event-study, or placebo reform dates, because the headline result is obviously
correct.

6. Conclusion

Minimum wage increases destroy teen employment. Policymakers everywhere should
take note: our county panel settles the debate.
